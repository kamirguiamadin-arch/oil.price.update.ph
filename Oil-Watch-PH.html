The short version

Almost all of the Philippines’ oil is reported to come from the Middle East. Since late February, fighting there has made that oil harder and more expensive to ship.

What is getting pricier

How fast prices rose in September, compared with a year earlier

The white mark is 4%, the top of the government’s target. Transport prices are rising more than three times faster than that. A government think tank estimates the crisis could push 1.3 to 3.1 million Filipinos into poverty.

How we got here

  1. Feb 28US and Israeli strikes on Iran. Iran declares the Strait of Hormuz closed. The Philippines had 55 to 57 days of fuel stock.
  2. Mar 16The LTFRB approves a jeepney fare hike as diesel climbs.
  3. Mar 24The President declares a national energy emergency, the first country to do so over this war.
  4. Mar 25₱20 billion from the Malampaya fund goes to buying fuel.
  5. Apr 13Taxes on LPG and kerosene are removed, which lowers their price by about ₱3.36 and ₱5.60 a liter.
  6. May 13Rotating brownouts leave nearly 2 million people without power for 2 to 3 hours at a time.
  7. Jun 18The central bank (BSP) raises its rate to 4.75% to slow price increases.
  8. Aug 7The economy’s growth for Apr–Jun is announced at 2.3%. People spent less. Transport groups ask for a ₱1 to ₱2 fare increase and plan a strike on Aug 10–12.
  9. Aug 27The BSP raises its rate again, to 5.00%.
  10. Oct 6Prices overall are up 7.2% in a year, more than experts expected.
  11. Oct 13Gas goes up ₱1.56 a liter and diesel goes down ₱3.01. The DOE says October fuel supply is secure.

What to watch next

  • BSP meeting this month. One bank expects rate hikes in October and December, taking the rate to 5.50%. Higher rates make loans and credit cards cost more.
  • Fuel for November and December. The DOE is checking if more diesel and kerosene shipments are needed.
  • The G7 diesel release. About 2.25 million barrels now, 3.75 million in November and 7.5 million after that.
  • The Strait of Hormuz. Analysts expect oil prices to jump right away if US–Iran fighting starts again.
  • Jeepney and bus fares. Transport groups are still asking for a temporary fare increase.

Words explained

Strait of Hormuz

A narrow sea passage between Iran and Oman. About a fifth of the world’s oil normally ships through it.

Inflation

How fast prices are rising. 7.2% means the same basket of goods costs 7.2% more than a year ago.

BSP and the policy rate

The BSP is the Philippines’ central bank. Its policy rate guides what banks charge for loans. Raising it slows spending and eases price rises, but loans cost more.

Economic growth (GDP)

How much bigger the economy got. The government aimed for 3.5–4.5% this year, so 2.3% is slow.

Brent and WTI

The two most quoted world oil prices. Brent is the global one, WTI is the US one. Both are in US dollars per barrel.

RON 91, 95, 97

The octane rating of gasoline. Higher numbers cost more and suit some engines. Check your vehicle’s manual.
